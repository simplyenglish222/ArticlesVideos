Retelling support

Open the answers only after watching. Use the suggested collocations to build your own retelling first.

1. Why was food preservation such an important problem in the past?
Useful collocations: keep food for the lean days · preserve food · a balanced diet · fresh fruits and vegetables · primitive methods
In the past, people could not keep food for long periods, especially when food was out of season or hunting was poor. Early preservation methods such as drying, smoking and salting helped, but they were limited and sometimes caused important vitamins to be lost. As a result, people often had difficulty maintaining a balanced diet throughout the year.
2. How did modern canning begin, and what was the key discovery?
Useful collocations: offer a prize · a safe method of preserving food · seal food in a container · thoroughly heated · perfect canning methods
The French government offered a prize for a safe method of preserving food. A French chef won it after discovering that food could be kept for years if it was tightly sealed in a container and then thoroughly heated. This became the basis of modern canning.
3. How did the canning industry develop after the invention of the can?
Useful collocations: set up a cannery · be done by hand · machines were doing more · reduce the cooking period · the price of canned food went down
At first, canning was slow because much of the work was done by hand. Later, machines made production much faster. New inventions such as the retort reduced cooking time from hours to minutes. As production became more efficient, canned food became cheaper and more widely available.
4. What happens to food in a modern cannery?
Useful collocations: ready to be processed · be husked or peeled · be sorted by size and ripeness · undergo frequent washings · be chopped or sliced · be blanched · be checked and tested · be labelled and shipped
After the crops arrive at the cannery, they are prepared for processing. They may be husked or peeled, sorted by size and ripeness, washed, chopped or sliced, and then blanched. The containers are filled, sealed and heated. Samples are checked and tested, and the finished cans are labelled and shipped.
5. According to the documentary, how did canning improve everyday life?
Useful collocations: a constant supply of good food · three square meals a day · all mankind · improve fruits and vegetables · better quality foods · meet special needs
The documentary argues that canning helped provide a more regular supply of food throughout the year. It also connected canning with improvements in agriculture, food quality and special products such as baby food. In the film’s view, this contributed to better nutrition and healthier lives.
