Video check · answer from the report

These questions depend on details from the video. Try to use the suggested expressions before opening the model answer.

1. What kinds of food did the reporters find in Walmart’s bins, and why did some examples surprise them?

Use: best before datestill good to eatperfectly good foodthrow it out
They found vegetables, dairy products, baked goods, frozen food and other packaged products. Some items had not even reached their best-before dates, so the reporters were surprised that food which still looked good to eat had been thrown out.

2. What did the former Walmart manager say about food waste during a typical shift?

Use: a shopping cart worth of foodfood waste policiescommon practice
He said that roughly a shopping cart worth of food could be thrown away during a shift. He also suggested that this was connected to store policy and was not an isolated event.

3. What happened after Marketplace told Walmart about the food found in its bins?

Use: take actionbehind lock and keyreduce how much is thrown out
After Marketplace contacted Walmart, the bins at some stores were placed behind lock and key. Walmart also said it was working to reduce how much food was thrown out.

4. What did Rob Greenfield’s dumpster-diving experiment show?

Use: dumpster divingtip of the icebergperfectly good foodmake a meal
His experiment showed that large quantities of food which still appeared edible could be found behind supermarkets. The group quickly collected enough food to make a meal, suggesting that what they saw might only be the tip of the iceberg.

5. Why does Rob Greenfield think supermarkets should donate food rather than throw it away?

Use: donate good foodpeople in needtake that extra stepgoing hungry
He argues that supermarkets should take the extra step and donate good food because many people are in need or going hungry. He believes that an advanced society should not waste edible food while people lack enough to eat.

6. Why can throwing food away be easier for a supermarket than donating it?

Use: cost more moneyone extra stepa little bit more effort
The report says that donation can cost more money and require one extra step. Organising the transfer of food to charities takes a little more effort than simply throwing the food away.

7. What did Walmart admit when the company finally agreed to an interview?

Use: address food wasteon the right tracka lot of work to dotake steps
Walmart admitted that mistakes were being made and said it wanted to reduce good food going to waste. The company said it was taking steps to address food waste, believed it was on the right track, but still had a lot of work to do.

8. How did the report compare Canada with countries such as France and Italy?

Use: a tax breakadopted food policiescracking down onway behind
France had introduced rules requiring supermarkets to give food to charities, while Italy offered a tax break to grocers who donated food. The report suggested that Canada was still way behind countries that had already adopted food-waste policies.

9. What did the supermarket experiment with ordinary shoppers reveal about household food waste?

Use: way more than we needtoss outtake food for grantedvalue food
The experiment showed that people often buy more than they need and later toss out part of it. Shoppers also seemed surprised by the amount they typically waste.

10. What lesson did the family take from visiting the waste facility?

Use: field tripon a daily basistake a lot for grantedthink before you buythink before you waste
The field trip showed the family how much food is discarded on a daily basis. They realised that people take food for granted, and the report ended with the message to think before you buy and think before you waste.

Discussion questions

Use the suggested collocations in your answer. Sample answers stay hidden.

1. Why do supermarkets throw away food that still seems good to eat?

best-before datesafe for consumptionfood waste policiesthrow food away
Supermarkets may throw food away because of safety rules, damaged packaging or internal food waste policies, even when some products still seem good to eat.

2. Should supermarkets be required to donate edible food instead of throwing it away?

donate food to shelterspeople in needtake an extra stepgo to waste
I think supermarkets should donate edible food to shelters because it could help people in need and prevent good food from going to waste.

3. What does dumpster diving reveal about the scale of food waste?

go dumpster divingperfectly good foodthe tip of the icebergend up in landfill
Dumpster diving can reveal large amounts of perfectly good food. What is visible in one dumpster may only be the tip of the iceberg.

4. Who should take the greatest responsibility: supermarkets, consumers or governments?

take action on food wasteadopt a food policyreduce food wastetighten up a process
Responsibility should be shared, but governments can take action by adopting clear food policies while retailers tighten up their processes.

5. Do best-before dates make consumers and shops waste more food than necessary?

a best-before datestill good to eatsafe for consumptiongo to waste
They can contribute to waste when people confuse quality dates with safety dates and discard food that is still good to eat.

6. Why do households often waste food?

buy more than we needthrow food awaytake food for grantedreduce food waste
Households often buy more than they need, forget what they already have, and take food for granted because it is easily available.

7. Is throwing food away ever the cheapest option but still the wrong decision?

take an extra stepdonate food to shelterspeople in needperfectly good food
It may be cheaper for a business to throw food away, but donating perfectly good food can be more socially responsible.

8. Should governments crack down on supermarket food waste?

crack down on food wasteadopt a food policytake action on food wasteend up in landfill
Governments could crack down on food waste by requiring large retailers to report waste and by making donation easier.

9. What practical steps could you take at home to waste less food?

buy more than we needa best-before datereduce food wastetake food for granted
I could plan meals, check what I already have, understand best-before dates better and avoid buying more than I need.

10. Is food waste mainly an economic, environmental or ethical problem?

billions of dollars’ worth of foodpeople in needend up in landfillgo to waste
It is all three: food waste costs money, creates unnecessary environmental pressure, and is difficult to justify while people are in need.
